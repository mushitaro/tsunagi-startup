When a project conversation starts, the first thing we talk about is not features. It is "who does this task today, and how." Capturing the existing workflow correctly matters more than adding clever features — and it is what leads to an app people keep using.

## "Would be nice" vs. "stuck right now"

Ideas for new features are endless. What you actually need is the task that happens every day and costs a little effort every time. Removing a small daily friction is worth far more, and for far longer, than an ideal feature used once a month.

- How often does the task happen each week?
- How many minutes does it take now?
- What happens when it goes wrong?

If an idea cannot answer those three questions, we consider it too early to build.

## Start small, then grow it through operation

The first release succeeds more often the fewer features it has. The reason is simple: smaller ships sooner, and reaches real users sooner, so feedback comes back sooner. Every tsunagi app started as "the smallest form I use every day" and grew from there.

Rather than aiming for perfection at the design stage, make the structure something you can fix after release. That is what separates an app that lasts from one that ends the day it ships.

## Estimate the cost of running it — first

An app spends far longer being kept alive than being built. So at the design stage we decide who maintains it, where the data lives, and how we notice when something breaks.

tsunagi defaults to static sites and serverless, choosing setups where operational effort and cost stay small. We prefer the choice that will not trouble us — or whoever inherits it — six months later, over the flashy one.

## Summary

Apps people keep using come from the right question, not the best feature. Make "who is stuck, and how" explicit, ship small, and grow it through operation. tsunagi Consulting begins exactly here — sorting out that starting point together.
